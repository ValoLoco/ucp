# Overview

This page summarizes Universal Company Protocol (UCP) using only claims already stated in README.md, STATUS.md, and [decision 0001](decisions/0001-research-first.md). For authoritative scope and corrections, read [../STATUS.md](../STATUS.md) and [research/assumptions-and-corrections.md](research/assumptions-and-corrections.md).

## What UCP is (as described in the repo)

UCP is presented as a **Bitcoin-native protocol** research effort aimed at portable legal entities, capital tables, commercial contracts, arbitration, reputation, settlement, and governance **beyond Earth** (README.md). It is developed **publicly** and discussed on **BUENATURA** live streams (README.md, STATUS.md).

The repository holds a **concept draft v0.1** under active public development (README.md). STATUS.md states UCP is an **early-stage research proposal** with **no** claimed participating jurisdictions, institutional endorsements, legal recognition, deployed protocol, or production readiness.

**Bitcoin-first** is the stated research direction (STATUS.md, decision 0001). The **seven layers** in README.md and `docs/architecture.md` are described as a **problem map**, not seven products to build immediately (STATUS.md).

The **first milestone** is a **signed, portable entity record** with **independently verifiable history**. That milestone does **not** create a legal person or issue securities (STATUS.md). Decision 0001 aligns: start with a portable, signed entity record; keep identity, legal recognition, contract interpretation, payment execution, and dispute enforcement distinct.

Stated **principles** (README.md, CONTRIBUTING.md) include maximum strength with minimum weight, building only what a real company needs now, one machine-readable representation for contracts, code, and legal state, and using Bitcoin for settlement finality, timestamping, and a neutral base layer where the research supports it.

Licensing in README.md proposes **CC-BY-4.0** for specifications and **MIT** for reference implementations; CONTRIBUTING.md notes that exact scope and contribution terms remain to be finalized.

## What UCP is not (as described in the repo)

STATUS.md explicitly denies jurisdictional commitments, endorsements, legal recognition, a deployed protocol, production readiness, and a **protocol token**.

Decision 0001 says the initial demonstrator has **no mandatory** Stacks dependency, tokenized cap table, protocol token, or on-chain arbitration; later layers remain **research topics**.

README.md and architecture material describe **aspirational** layers and technology choices. STATUS.md says those initial documents are **not** verified capabilities or commitments, and that Stacks, ION, Taproot Assets, RGB, Lightning, and BitVM are **candidates to evaluate**, not required dependencies.

CONTRIBUTING.md warns against claiming endorsement or recognition without evidence, and against treating merges or live-stream discussion as legal recognition.

The concept article in `content/articles/` frames UCP as an **open framework to investigate**, not a government, and states that **legal recognition** stays a separate question for relevant institutions (editorial draft; fact checking still required per that file).
