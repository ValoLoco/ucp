# Assumptions and corrections

## Legal boundaries

The absence of a universal incorporation regime does not imply a legal vacuum. Existing corporate law, contractual choice of law, arbitration, regulation, and international space law must be examined before proposing replacements.

A cryptographic identifier does not confer legal personality. A token does not automatically constitute a legally effective share register. A credential proves an issuer made a claim, not that a court accepts it.

Recognition by states, registrars, courts, and regulators is a proposed research path. No agreements exist within this project. Safety zones must not be described as automatically creating property rights.

Code can implement explicit rules but cannot exhaust fiduciary duties, interpret every contract, or replace due process. Arbitration enforcement is jurisdiction-dependent and must be assessed by qualified counsel.

## Technical boundaries

Bitcoin confirmation confidence is not absolute finality. Anchoring establishes a commitment under stated verification assumptions, not the truth, legality, authorship, or availability of its contents.

Stacks and other adjacent networks introduce their own assumptions. They must not be presented as equivalent to direct Bitcoin execution. BitVM verification is not a court for deciding real-world disputes.

A Taproot Assets universe is not a legal jurisdiction. Securities restrictions and cross-network controls need an explicit, tested design. Stablecoin availability and issuer support must be checked rather than assumed.

Ten-minute blocks do not establish interplanetary suitability. Communication delay, network partitions, mining distribution, reorganizations, data availability, channel monitoring, and dispute windows need separate analysis. Lightning is not presumed suitable for Earth-Mars settlement.

## Evidence process

For every external claim, record a primary source, access date, relevant passage, limitations, and reviewer. Track statements as verified fact, proposal, assumption, or open question. Public articles must distinguish these categories.
