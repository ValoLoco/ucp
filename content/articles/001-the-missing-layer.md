# The Missing Layer: Who Can Do Business Beyond Earth?

By Valentin Kranz, BUENATURA

Editorial status: concept article draft. Primary-source fact checking and legal review remain required before publication.

To Elon Musk and the teams at SpaceX, to builders of AI systems, and to everyone developing the next generation of space infrastructure: what commercial foundations should independent actors share beyond Earth?

Transport makes arrival possible. Cooperation makes an economy possible. We should design for both.

## A company that can travel

Imagine an equipment supplier serving customers on Earth, in orbit, and eventually on Mars. Who can bind the company? Which record establishes ownership? What happens when a signing key is compromised? Which law governs a disagreement, and who can enforce the outcome?

These are design questions for a future commercial system, not claims that existing law disappears beyond Earth.

Our proposal is to investigate a Universal Company Protocol: an open framework for portable company records, verifiable authority, and cooperation across legal and technical boundaries.

## A protocol, not a government

UCP should not claim territory, invent recognition, or declare itself above national law. Its first task should be narrower: make it easier to establish what an organization claims to be, who may act for it, and how its records have changed.

Legal recognition would remain a separate question for the relevant institutions. Cryptographic verification would provide evidence, not sovereignty.

## Why investigate Bitcoin?

We propose evaluating Bitcoin as an optional commitment and settlement foundation. The research question is where it adds independently verifiable evidence or useful settlement capability, and where a signed document or existing standard is enough.

We will not assume that every record needs a blockchain, that every share should become a token, or that Earth-based payment assumptions work across interplanetary distances.

Bitcoin-first should mean disciplined evaluation, not technology by slogan.

## AI needs bounded authority

In this concept, an AI system acting for a company would receive explicit, limited, revocable permissions. The protocol should make the responsible principal visible and preserve a reviewable authorization history.

We do not propose granting AI systems legal personality as a prerequisite. Delegation and accountability are useful problems to investigate first.

## Start smaller than the vision

The initial demonstrator should create a fictional entity record, verify authorized changes, rotate a compromised key, and detect conflicting histories. An optional Bitcoin commitment can follow once those basics work.

Ownership, contracting, dispute resolution, and interplanetary operation remain later research areas, each requiring its own legal and technical review.

## An open invitation

SpaceX and Elon Musk are natural participants in this discussion, but the design should not depend on one founder or company. We invite space operators, AI developers, Bitcoin engineers, corporate registries, commercial lawyers, arbitrators, insurers, researchers, and prospective users to challenge it.

What already solves this problem? What must remain local? What should never be automated? Where does a shared protocol genuinely reduce friction?

We will develop these questions publicly through BUENATURA articles and YouTube live streams. The repository is https://github.com/ValoLoco/ucp.

The ambition is large. The next step should be small, testable, and useful.
